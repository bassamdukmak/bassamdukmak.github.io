The governing board of Ohio Ihsan Academy (OIA) has adopted the following racial nondiscriminatory policies:

The OIA school recruits and admits students of any race, color or ethnic origin to all its rights, privileges, programs and activities. In addition, the school will not discriminate on the basis of race, color, national and ethnic origin in administration of its educational policies, admissions policies, employment, scholarship and loan programs, and athletic and other school administered programs.

Ohio EdChoice Scholarship Program

Ohio Ihsan Academy is a chartered nonpublic school recognized by the State of Ohio and is an approved provider for the Ohio EdChoice Scholarship Program. Students in Kindergarten and above may apply for and use an Ohio EdChoice Scholarship toward the cost of tuition at OIA, subject to eligibility requirements and approval by the State of Ohio.

Families are responsible for completing all required scholarship application materials and submitting any necessary documentation by the established deadlines.

Families who receive an EdChoice Scholarship remain responsible for any tuition balance, fees, or charges not covered by the scholarship award. Scholarship funds are applied directly toward tuition only and do not replace a family’s obligation to comply with OIA’s tuition payment policies, enrollment requirements, and financial agreements.

How EdChoice Is Applied to Tuition at OIA

Ohio Ihsan Academy is committed to ensuring that high-quality education remains accessible to families. The EdChoice Scholarship is a valuable resource that helps many families afford tuition; however, EdChoice payments from the State of Ohio begin later in the school year (typically October or November), while tuition billing begins in July.

To balance affordability for families with our responsibility for sound financial stewardship, OIA has established the following policies:

Payment Plans A & B (Annual and Semester Plans)

Families who choose to pay their tuition upfront (either for the full year or by semester) and receive the associated discount will continue to follow the regular payment schedule.

  • EdChoice funds are applied to your account as the state sends them to the school during the year.
  • Refunds for Plans A and B will be issued only at the end of the school year (June) once all EdChoice payments have been received and reconciled.
  • This allows us to ensure accuracy and protects the school in case of changes to eligibility, state delays, or withdrawals.

Payment Plan C (Monthly Payments)

Many families who select the monthly payment plan rely on the EdChoice Scholarship to make enrollment at OIA possible. To support these families:

  • OIA applies the expected EdChoice award amount as a tuition credit at the start of the payment cycle in July.
  • This immediately reduces the family’s monthly tuition payments for the school year.
  • Program fees and other non-tuition charges are separate and must be paid in full, as EdChoice funds may only be applied toward tuition.

If a scholarship amount changes, eligibility changes, payments are delayed, or a student withdraws, OIA will update the family’s account and payment schedule accordingly.

This approach ensures that families who depend on the scholarship are able to begin the year with manageable monthly payments while allowing OIA to maintain responsible financial practices.

LEARNING AT OIA

Grade Levels

Montessori (Preschool - Kindergarten)

The Montessori approach is both a philosophy and a method of education designed to nurture independence, curiosity, concentration, and a lifelong love of learning. In a prepared classroom environment, students use hands-on materials to explore concepts at their own pace, develop practical life skills, solve problems, and make meaningful connections across areas of learning. Montessori teachers are trained to observe each child closely and provide lessons based on the child’s developmental readiness, interests, and needs. Through individual and small-group instruction, children move from concrete, hands-on experiences toward deeper understanding and abstract thinking.

The main areas of the Montessori curriculum include Practical Life, Sensorial, Language, and Mathematics. Practical Life activities build independence, focus, coordination, responsibility, and care for self and environment, while incorporating basic Islamic manners and etiquette. Sensorial materials help students refine their senses and develop order, sequence, and directionality, including right-to-left orientation in Arabic when appropriate. Language instruction uses hands-on materials to build phonemic awareness, vocabulary, early reading, and writing skills. Mathematics begins with concrete understanding before moving to symbols, as students explore counting, number sense, place value, and the decimal system through Montessori materials.

At OIA, the Montessori curriculum supports the development of the whole child while integrating Islamic values, Quran, and Arabic language. Islamic Studies is woven throughout the Montessori day through modeling, hands-on activities, stories, songs, and connections to the curriculum. Students are introduced to a love for Allah, His Messenger ﷺ, and our deen in an age-appropriate and meaningful way. A Quran teacher also works with Montessori students individually or in small groups to support age-appropriate recitation and memorization.

Arabic, Qur'an and Islamic Studies

The students are taught Fiqh, Seerah, Tafseer and Aqidah to instill an understanding of Islam, a desire to practice Islam, an appreciation of the Qur'an as the Guide to mankind. These three courses constitute the “Rope of Islam” to hold onto and, Insha’Allah, to lead to success in the hereafter.

At Ohio Ihsan Academy, students benefit from a holistic education that combines rigorous academic knowledge with comprehensive Islamic studies and spirituality. Our integrated approach ensures that Islamic studies is seamlessly woven into core subjects, allowing students to experience Islam throughout their day. Arabic is taught as a second language to aid in understanding the Qur'an and Hadith, empowering students to derive accurate knowledge from these foundational Islamic texts.

Through academic knowledge and Islamic knowledge students gain the necessary threads to become a strong and confident Muslim. This interconnected education equips students with the skills and values needed to succeed academically and spiritually, guiding them towards success in this life and the hereafter.

TECHNOLOGY, DIGITAL SAFETY, AND ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

Ohio Ihsan Academy recognizes technology as a valuable tool for teaching and learning. Consistent with OIA’s mission to cultivate the holistic development of each child, the School approaches technology as an amanah, or trust, that must be exercised with responsibility, wisdom, balance, and integrity.

Students, staff, families, and school leaders share responsibility for ensuring that technology protects children, supports beneficial learning, preserves healthy development, and upholds Islamic character and values. OIA prioritizes foundational learning, critical thinking, reading, writing, problem-solving, creativity, student independence, and healthy development. Technology and artificial intelligence may support instruction and school operations, but may not replace student effort, teacher judgment, direct instruction, hands-on learning, meaningful human interaction, or human review.

Student Technology Use

Students may use school technology only for approved educational purposes, under the direction and supervision of OIA staff, and in accordance with teacher instructions. Student device use is limited to 30 minutes per school day unless otherwise approved by administration.

Grade-Level Technology Expectations

Preschool–Kindergarten: Technology use will be minimal and primarily teacher-directed. Students will focus on hands-on learning, exploration, social development, and foundational academic skills.

Grades 1–2: Students may use technology for teacher-assigned educational programs, skill practice, and other approved learning activities.

Students in Preschool through Grade 2 may not independently use AI tools for school assignments, classroom activities, research, writing, images, or projects. Teachers may discuss AI in age-appropriate ways, including explaining that some computer tools can generate answers, pictures, or suggestions, but that students must still use their own thinking and effort.

Grades 3–5: Students may have grade-appropriate opportunities to use technology for research, projects, presentations, typing and writing assignments, and digital organization.

Students will receive instruction in evaluating online information, digital responsibility, and appropriate technology use. In Grade 3, research and internet use must remain highly teacher-directed. Independent internet searching is not permitted unless explicitly approved and supervised by the teacher.

Artificial Intelligence Use

AI is primarily a staff and teacher support tool at OIA. Because OIA serves young learners, students may not independently use AI tools at school or for school assignments completed at home unless a teacher or administrator has explicitly approved their use for a specific assignment or instructional activity.

If AI use is not explicitly allowed, students should assume it is not permitted.

Academic Integrity

Students must submit work that reflects their own thinking, effort, and understanding. Using AI or another technology tool to complete work that a student is expected to do independently is considered misuse.

School-Owned Devices

School devices remain the property of Ohio Ihsan Academy. Users must handle devices carefully, keep food and drinks away from technology, store and charge devices appropriately, and immediately report damage or problems.

Students and families may be responsible for repair or replacement costs when damage results from intentional misuse, neglect, or failure to follow school expectations.

School devices may be inspected, monitored, or reviewed by administration at any time.

Family Communication

Because students may encounter AI and other digital tools at home and online, OIA may provide families with age-appropriate guidance regarding responsible AI use, digital citizenship, academic honesty, privacy, and safe technology habits.

Families are encouraged to supervise student technology use at home and discuss the importance of honesty, independent thinking, and responsible online behavior.

Violations and Consequences

Misuse of technology or AI may result in consequences including

  • Reteaching of expectations
  • Parent notification
  • Redoing an assignment
  • Loss of technology privileges
  • Restitution for damages
  • Administrative action or other consequences under school policy

Consequences will take into account the student’s age, intent, the severity of the incident, and previous behavior.

CLASSROOM MANAGEMENT AND STUDENT DISCIPLINE POLICY

At Ohio Ihsan Academy, we strive to cultivate an Islamic environment that is safe and conducive to learning, guided by the teachings of the Qur'an and the Sunnah of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. In line with the Hadith that "The best of you are those with the most excellent character and are careful of those around them," we emphasize the importance of Islamic conduct and self-control in all aspects of school life.

Students are expected to follow the rules, as outlined in the Student Responsibilities section. Islamic conduct and self-control are stressed in all classes and monitored by the Administration and all other staff members.

Ohio Ihsan Academy does not tolerate behavior that disrupts or interferes with the education of other students or the school learning environment. Students are responsible for understanding and adhering to this code of conduct. Parents are responsible for helping students understand and abide by these policies, for recognizing that unacceptable behavior is subject to disciplinary action, and for supporting the enforcement of these policies.

It is the policy of Ohio Ihsan Academy that no physical punishment will be used. Any parent who suspects that physical punishment has been used is encouraged to bring this to the attention of the Administration (admin@oiacademy.org) and Principal (nazek.hapasha@oiacademy.org) in an email.

Teachers will manage student behavior in the classroom, using their classroom management plan as a first step. If a student’s behavior disrupts the learning environment or violates the Student Responsibilities despite the teacher's intervention, the student will be referred to the Administration for further review and action.

The following actions are a part of our progressive discipline process, which is designed to uphold high standards while ensuring fairness, clarity, and accountability.

How Behavior Is Addressed

Teachers are the first line of support for managing student behavior in the classroom. Most minor issues are handled directly by teachers using classroom strategies. However, if behavior becomes serious or continues despite multiple interventions, it may be referred to the school administration.

We group behaviors into two broad categories

Tier 1: Teacher-Managed (Minor Infractions)

Examples

  • Talking out of turn or being disruptive
  • Not following directions
  • Minor disrespect (e.g., eye-rolling, unkind tone)
  • Inappropriate language (not directed at others)
  • Minor dishonesty
  • Minor peer conflict
  • Lack of preparedness
  • Touching others’ belongings or pushing
  • Being off-task or disengaged

Teachers use a range of strategies to help students improve behavior, such as

  • Verbal redirection
  • Visual cues or gentle reminders
  • Break cards or movement breaks
  • Behavior reflection sheets (“Fix-It Tickets”)
  • Private conversations
  • Earning privileges for positive changes

If the behavior continues, the teacher may

  • Contact parents
  • Create a behavior improvement plan
  • Schedule a meeting with parents and administration

Office-Managed Infractions

Some behaviors are more serious and require immediate involvement from the school administration. These include both moderate and severe infractions:

Tier 2: Moderate Infractions

Examples

  • Repeated defiance or disrespect
  • Repeated inappropriate language
  • Verbal altercations
  • Bullying (patterned or repeated)
  • Misuse of school technology
  • Minor property damage
  • Repeated cheating or dishonesty
  • Stealing

Possible Consequences

  • Behavior contracts
  • Parent meetings
  • Restorative assignments (e.g., apology letters, community service)
  • Suspension (1 or more days)
  • Possible expulsion for repeated offenses

Tier 3: Severe Infractions

Examples

  • Physical aggression or causing harm to others
  • Intentional destruction of school property
  • Possession or use of drugs, alcohol, or weapons
  • Sexual harassment
  • Threats, hate speech, or cyberbullying
  • Sharing explicit content
  • Directed profanity or verbal abuse

Possible Consequences

  • Immediate removal from class
  • Parent contact and formal investigation
  • Suspension pending review
  • Reentry meeting and behavior plan
  • Expulsion (pending board review)

ATTENDANCE

Ohio Ihsan Academy believes that regular attendance and punctuality are essential to student success. Consistent attendance allows students to fully participate in classroom learning, build relationships, and benefit from the academic and spiritual environment of the school.

Families and the school share responsibility for supporting strong attendance habits. OIA monitors attendance by instructional time missed, including full-day absences, partial-day absences, late arrivals, early dismissals, and time away from instruction during the school day.

Reporting An Absence

Please report your child’s absence in advance whenever possible by calling or emailing the office. The office will notify the teacher.

If a student is absent and the parent/guardian was unable to notify the school on the day of the absence, the parent/guardian should send a written note or email to the office on the day the student returns. This documentation is required to determine whether the absence will be marked as excused or unexcused.

The note should include

  • The student’s full name
  • The date(s) of the absence
  • The reason for the absence
  • The parent or guardian’s signature (or full name if sent by email)

If a student is absent for more than one day, parents are encouraged to reach out directly to the student’s teacher(s) to support their child in making up missed assignments and classwork. Teachers will work with families to ensure students stay on track academically and have access to the materials they need.

Please note that extended or frequent absences may require additional documentation and communication with school administration.

Types Of Absences

Excused Absences

Absences may be excused for reasons recognized by the school, including but not limited to

  • Student illness
  • Medical or dental appointments
  • Illness or emergency within the family
  • Funeral or bereavement
  • Required court appearances
  • Approved family travel
  • Religious observances
  • Other circumstances approved by school administration

Documentation may be required for extended or repeated absences.

Students with excused absences will be provided the opportunity to complete missed assignments and classroom work.

Unexcused Absences

An absence may be considered unexcused when

  • The reason for the absence does not meet the criteria for an excused absence.
  • Required communication or documentation is not provided.
  • A student misses school without parent/guardian knowledge or approval.
  • Family travel occurs without approval or exceeds approved limits.

Unexcused absences are tracked and may contribute to habitual truancy thresholds under Ohio attendance requirements.

Chronic Absence and Habitual Truancy

Ohio attendance requirements are based on the number of instructional hours missed rather than the number of school days missed.

Chronic Absence

A student may be considered chronically absent when the student misses 10% or more of the minimum required instructional hours for the school year. Chronic absence includes time missed for any reason, including excused absences, unexcused absences, late arrivals, early dismissals, and partial-day absences.

Because all missed instructional time affects learning, OIA will monitor attendance regularly and communicate with families when attendance patterns begin to interfere with a student’s academic progress, classroom participation, or school routine.

OIA will notify parents/guardians no later than when a student has missed 5% of the minimum required instructional hours for the school year. The school may communicate concerns earlier when patterns of absence, tardiness, or early dismissal begin to develop.

Habitual Truancy

A student may be considered habitually truant under Ohio law when unexcused absences reach the following thresholds:

  • 30 or more consecutive hours without a legitimate excuse;
  • 42 or more hours in one school month without a legitimate excuse; or
  • 72 or more hours in one school year without a legitimate excuse.

When a student approaches or meets attendance concern thresholds, OIA may require parent/guardian communication, additional documentation, an attendance conference, an attendance support plan, or other interventions to improve attendance.

Attendance Support and Intervention Process

The goal of OIA’s attendance process is to support students and families, identify barriers to attendance, and reduce missed instructional time.

When attendance concerns arise, OIA may use a tiered support process that includes

  • Parent/guardian communication regarding attendance concerns;
  • Teacher or administrator check-ins;
  • Review of attendance patterns and possible barriers;
  • Requests for documentation when appropriate;
  • Development of an attendance support plan;
  • Referral to appropriate school or community supports;
  • An attendance support conference with administration

The school will focus on communication, support, documentation, and intervention to improve attendance.

Family Travel

While family trips can provide meaningful experiences, regular attendance is essential for student learning. Families are encouraged to schedule travel during school breaks whenever possible.

Family travel during the school year requires prior approval from administration.

  • Requests should be submitted at least two weeks in advance.
  • Each student may receive approval for up to five (5) school days of family travel per year without academic penalty, provided missed assignments are completed.
  • Multiple trips totaling five (5) or fewer school days may be approved at the discretion of administration.
  • Trips exceeding five (5) school days will generally not be approved except under extraordinary circumstances.

Approved family travel may be marked excused but still counts as missed instructional time. Unapproved or extended travel may be recorded as unexcused and may count toward attendance thresholds under Ohio law and school policy.

Regular tuition obligations continue during all absences, whether excused or unexcused.

School Activities

School-sponsored instructional activities, including field trips and approved programs, are considered part of the educational experience and do not count as absences.

Students participating in school-sponsored activities are responsible for completing any missed classroom assignments.

Early Dismissal

Parents/guardians should notify the office when a student needs to leave school early. Students must be signed out through the school office by an authorized adult before leaving the building.

Any instructional time missed due to early dismissal will be documented as part of the student’s attendance record. Frequent early dismissals may result in communication with administration to ensure students are receiving adequate instructional time.

Tardiness Policy

Ohio Ihsan Academy places a high value on punctuality and regular attendance. The first minutes of the school day are essential for establishing routines, building community, and beginning academic instruction. Chronic tardiness disrupts learning for the tardy student, the teacher, and the entire class.

Students may arrive beginning at 8:30 a.m.

Students should be in their classrooms and prepared to begin the school day no later than 8:40 a.m.

Students arriving after 8:40 a.m. will be marked tardy and must be signed in through the school office by a parent/guardian.

Excused Tardies

Tardies may be excused for reasons such as

  • Medical or dental appointments
  • Severe weather or traffic accidents
  • Other circumstances approved by the School

Documentation may be required.

Unexcused Tardies

Examples of unexcused tardies include, but are not limited to

  • Oversleeping
  • Missing the bus or alarm
  • Running late
  • Transportation delays within the family's control
  • Failure to leave home on time

Tardy Support and Intervention Process

Ohio Ihsan Academy recognizes that occasional delays happen. The goal of monitoring tardiness is not to penalize families for isolated circumstances, but to address patterns of missed instructional time and support consistent attendance.

OIA will monitor tardiness on an ongoing basis. When patterns of frequent tardiness occur, the school may take the following steps:

  • 5 Tardies in a Month: Parents/guardians will receive communication from the school regarding the importance of timely arrival and the impact of missed instructional time.
  • 8 Tardies in a Month: A parent/guardian meeting with administration may be required to discuss barriers to timely arrival and develop a plan for improvement.

Tardies and Missed Instructional time

If tardiness continues despite communication and support, additional interventions may be implemented. Chronic patterns of tardiness that significantly impact instructional time may be considered when making enrollment and re-enrollment decisions.

HEALTH AND SAFETY

First Aid/CPR

All staff members at the Ohio Ihsan Academy teaching students must have completed a First Aid/CPR class.

Medical Guidelines

If a child becomes ill during school, he/she will be cared for temporarily, and the parent will be notified. Simple first aid will be administered, but no medication will be given without prior signed permission.

Until the parent arrives, the student will be allowed to rest in the principal’s office. If the parent cannot be reached, and the illness is a mild headache, slight fever, stomachache, etc. the student will remain in the office until parents call back and arrange early dismissal. For serious illness or injuries, the student will be referred to a physician or hospital.

As a general rule, if your child is too ill to play outside with his/her class, or participate in regular classroom activities, then your child is too ill to attend school and should remain home.

Per licensing rules, a child is considered to be sick and should stay home and be excluded from school when demonstrating any of the following symptoms:

  • Temperature of at least one hundred and one degrees Fahrenheit (one hundred degrees Fahrenheit if taken axillary) when in combination with any other sign or symptom of illness.
  • Diarrhea (three or more abnormally, unexpectedly or unexplained loose stools within a twenty-four hour period).
  • Severe coughing, causing the child to become red or blue in the face or to make a whooping sound.
  • Difficult or rapid breathing.
  • Yellowish skin or eyes.
  • Redness of the eye or eyelid, thick and purulent (pus) eye discharge, matted eyelashes, burning, itching or eye pain.
  • Untreated infected skin patches, unusual spots or rashes.
  • Unusually dark urine and /or gray or white stool.
  • Stiff neck with elevated temperature.
  • Evidence of untreated lice, scabies, or other parasitic infestations.
  • Sore throat or difficulty in swallowing.
  • Vomiting more than one time or when accompanied by any other sign or symptom of illness.

If a student is ill at home with a contagious illness or fever, parents should not allow the student to come to school until they have been fever-free for 24 hours. If a child contracts a contagious disease such as strep throat, pink eye, or chicken pox, the school office must be informed immediately. Students with contagious diseases cannot return to school without a doctor’s note or until they are symptom-free. Please refer to the following chart for more details.

COVID

● Child should remain home and follow the most current CDC guidelines (call the school office for extra guidance).

● If there was a possibility that other students had contact, all parents will be informed.

Medicine & Medical Regulations

Most medications should be administered at home before or after school whenever possible. Ohio Ihsan Academy does not administer routine, convenience, or over-the-counter medication during the school day unless the medication is required as part of a completed health care plan signed by a medical provider.

OIA staff will not dispense over-the-counter medication, including but not limited to Tylenol, ibuprofen, cough medicine, cold medicine, vitamins, supplements, or similar products, unless the medication is specifically required as part of a student’s approved health care plan and all required documentation is on file.

If it is absolutely necessary for medication to be administered during the school day, such as a short-term prescription medication or antibiotic, the parent/guardian must provide written authorization from the child’s physician or authorized medical provider. The documentation must include the student’s name, the name of the medication, the dosage, the time or circumstances under which the medication must be administered, the start and end date, possible severe adverse reactions, emergency contact information for the medical provider, and any special instructions for administration or storage.

  • For Montessori students, additional child care licensing forms are required. If a Montessori student has a medical condition, allergy, emergency medication need, medical food need, or other health-related need that requires staff monitoring, staff action, medication administration, medical food administration, avoidance of specific triggers, trained procedures, or permission for the child to carry emergency medication, the parent/guardian must provide a completed JFS/DCY 01236 Child Medical/Physical Care Plan before care begins or immediately upon confirmation of the condition.
  • For Montessori students, any medication administration that is not already fully addressed in a completed JFS/DCY 01236 may also require the appropriate JFS/DCY 01217 medication authorization form before medication can be administered.

Medication brought to school must be delivered to the office by the parent/guardian in the original container and must be clearly labeled with the student’s name, the name of the medication, the dosage, and the administration instructions. Parents/guardians are responsible for monitoring the amount of medication at school and providing more as needed.

Medication will be stored securely by the school unless a student has written authorization to self-carry emergency medication. A medication log or record will be kept for medication administered at school.

For safety reasons, students may not bring medication to school or keep medication in their classroom, backpack, lunchbox, cubby, or personal belongings unless they have written authorization to self-carry emergency medication. Students may not share medication with another student.

Students may self-carry or self-administer emergency medication, including inhalers, epinephrine auto-injectors, diabetic supplies, seizure medication, or other emergency medication, only when permitted by law, authorized in writing by the parent/guardian and medical provider, approved by the school, and documented in the student’s file. The written authorization must include all required instructions, emergency contacts, and any information staff need to know in the event the medication does not produce the expected relief or the student is unable to self-administer.

To the fullest extent permitted by law, OIA, its administrators, teachers, staff, officers, and directors are not liable for injury, death, or loss allegedly arising from permitting or prohibiting a student’s use of self-carried emergency medication when acting in good faith and in accordance with the documentation on file.

OIA does not administer topical products, including sunscreen, lotions, ointments, or ChapStick/lip balm. Families should apply these products before school when needed.

Medication that is expired, discontinued, no longer needed, or not picked up by the parent/guardian will be discarded by the school at the end of the school year or when no longer authorized.

  • - A written statement from the student’s treating practitioner providing information on each drug prescribed to the student for a seizure disorder.

Montessori Licensing and Required Health Forms

Because the Montessori classroom is licensed under Ohio child care regulations, additional forms are required for Montessori students. These forms help OIA meet state licensing requirements and ensure that staff have the information needed to provide safe, appropriate care for each child.

Families are responsible for submitting all required forms by the stated deadlines and for keeping information current throughout the school year. Please contact the school office if you need assistance completing any forms or if you are unsure whether your child requires a care plan.

Enrollment Form — JFS 01234

The Enrollment Form, JFS 01234, is due before your child attends the program. This form collects essential information, including emergency contacts, authorized pick-up persons, and health alerts. The form must be updated annually. There is a box at the bottom of the last page for parents and the administrator to initial and date each year. This allows the form to be updated annually without needing a new form each year.

Child Medical Statement — JFS 01305

The Child Medical Statement, JFS 01305, is due within 30 days of enrollment. This form must be completed by your child’s healthcare provider. It verifies that your child is in good health and up to date on immunizations. The Child Medical Statement must be renewed every 13 months from the date of your child’s last medical exam.

Health Care Plan — JFS 01236, If Applicable

The Health Care Plan, JFS 01236, is required if your child has a medical condition such as asthma, allergies, diabetes, seizures, specific dietary needs, or another condition that requires specific care while at school.

If applicable, this form is due by your child’s first day of attendance.

The Health Care Plan provides staff with specific instructions for managing your child’s health needs while in care. Like the Enrollment Form, this form also has a section to be initialed and dated annually by both the parent and the administrator.

If your child has no medical conditions requiring a care plan, you do not need to complete the Health Care Plan form.

Modified Diets

Parents/guardians are responsible for providing all medical food, modified diets, and snacks required by their child. All food brought to school must comply with the dietary requirements specified by the healthcare provider.

Allergies

Parents are required to provide any medications, EpiPens, or other emergency medications that must be kept in the classroom or school office. Parents must also provide all required documentation and instructions for administration, and must train or review procedures with appropriate staff members as required.

Bathroom Use and Change of Clothing

All Montessori students must be fully toilet trained before attending school.

Each student is required to keep a change of clothing at school. If a change of clothes is needed, a teacher or staff member will guide the student on how to change and bag the soiled clothing to be sent home. Parents will be notified of such events.

Naptime and Rest Time

Students ages 3–4 will have nap time from 12:30 to 2:00, in accordance with Ohio Child Care Center regulations.

Injuries, Accidents, and Emergencies

Ensuring the safety of our students is our top priority, and we take every precaution to maintain a secure environment within our school.

In the event of a minor injury, an incident report will be completed and filed in the child’s record, with a copy provided to the parent.

For major injuries, accidents, or medical emergencies, parents will be notified immediately. If necessary, and if parents cannot be reached, emergency services will be contacted, and the child will be transported to the hospital promptly.

If a child is injured and requires medical attention, it is imperative that OIA have parent/guardian permission to contact local EMT for transportation to the nearest medical facility. We are unable to accept children whose parents refuse to grant consent for transportation to a source of emergency treatment.

According to ODJFS Rule 5180:2-12-16, a “Serious Incident, Injury or Illness” includes any situation occurring while a child is in care of the Center that requires emergency medical treatment, professional consultation, or transportation for emergency treatment.

Staff/Child Ratio and Supervision

Each classroom will consistently maintain a ratio of at least two certified teachers per 12 students, while adhering to our maximum class size of 18 students.

Continuous supervision by two teachers ensures that students are never left unsupervised under any circumstances.

SCHOOL VISITORS

Procedures for Visitors

Ohio Ihsan Academy welcomes visitors during school hours, but to ensure minimal disruption to academic activities, visitors are not permitted in classrooms. Upon arrival, visitors must report to the school office and acquire a visitor's pass. Additionally, before departing the campus, visitors are kindly requested to "sign out."

Classroom Visitation

To ensure an organized and a safe learning environment for our students, parents must seek administrative approval before visiting their child’s classroom. Classroom visits may be approved or denied at the discretion of the administration.

To ensure an optimal visit, parents should speak to the teacher/administration to find a mutually agreeable time for a visit that will not be disruptive to a classroom's routine or learning environment.

Young children are not permitted to visit classrooms with their parents, but may join their brother or sister during lunch with a parent. Parents should call the office before 10:00 a.m. if they plan to have lunch with their child(ren). When young children who do not attend the school accompany parents onto the school campus, they must be under parental supervision at all times.

ALL visitors are expected to wear modest clothing while on school campus.
